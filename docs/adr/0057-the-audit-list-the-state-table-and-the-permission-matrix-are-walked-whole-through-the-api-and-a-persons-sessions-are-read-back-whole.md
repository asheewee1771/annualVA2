# The audit list, the state table and the permission matrix are walked whole through the API, and a person's sessions are read back whole

Status: accepted (the filter ids in item 6 decided 2026-10-26, docs/agents/reviewer-decisions.md)

Ticket 50 closes the spec's Testing Decisions for story 84 (an audit entry for every action), the VA Submission state table (every row, rejected transitions included) and IM8 ac-1, ac-2 and lm-4 (every role against every endpoint, the step-up list, every refusal audited). It checks what tickets 02 to 49 built against the spec, fixes what the check found, and builds the one decision a human made during it (Q11).

1. **The state table is a table in the test** (`StateTableTest`). Each state, `NONE` (a System with no VA Submission) included, is reached through the real commands on SYS-F1; then every command the spec's table does not allow from it (20 commands: start, submit, request, review a Finding or a justification, both returns, pass, endorse, approve, reject, record and confirm a conditional VA, and the seven writes to prepared content) must answer `409 invalid-transition`, leave the state as it was and add one `REFUSED` entry of its action with a reason. Then the Overdue sweep runs at the end of week 2, week 4 and the Quarter, and the flag must be set exactly on the states the spec names. A second parameterised test makes each of the table's 34 moves and checks where it lands and its audit entry's `state_from` and `state_to`: every command move, the request from each state before Endorsed, a rejection back to Draft, In Review, Returned for Rework, Awaiting Endorsement and Not Started, the conditional Exemption's lapse back to Draft and to Not Started, the Register's withdrawal of each in-flight state and the Cycle's close of each non-final one. A Withdrawn VA Submission's System is retired, so its SMO/Tech Lead's and Cyber Manager's commands answer `404` (their roles for it were revoked, ADR 0049), and only the VA POCs' answer `409`.

2. **The audit list is one walk** (`AuditCompletenessTest`). One test factory drives a VA Cycle's life through the API (the Register, a VA Submission from start to endorsement through a Cyber Manager's and a VA POC's return, a malware rejection, Exemptions approved, rejected and confirmed, a withdrawal, a role-holder review, reads of the audit log, an export and its disposal, the Overdue sweep, the role lapse and return, the Cycle's open and close, sign-in, sign-out, a step-up and refusals), then produces one dynamic test per row: the entry's action and outcome, the actor and the role they held (none for a job), the System, VA Submission and Finding ids where it is about one, the fixed detail it must carry, and a correlation id. Every free text typed in the walk carries a sentinel that no entry may hold. Entries about a System are read by System; entries about none are told from the rest of the suite's by the ids that existed before the walk.

3. **The permission matrix is a table in the test** (`PermissionMatrixTest`): 63 endpoints, seven people (nobody signed in; the SMO/Tech Lead and Cyber Manager of SYS-F1 and of SYS-F2; a VA POC signed in 10 minutes ago; an Auditor), each cell `OK` (the access rules let it through and the command answers below 500, whatever it answers), `401`, `403`, `404` or `STEP` (`403 step-up-required` with the row's intent). Every `401` and `403` must be in the audit log as `REQUEST_REFUSED`, found by the response's correlation id, with its status, reason, intent and actor. The step-up list (nine intents) is checked stale and fresh. In Release 1 an Auditor opens only what every role may read; the Auditor's scope, the Cyber Manager's detailed export and the auditor pack are Release 2.

4. **Guards against a later gap.** Each suite fails the build when the code grows past it: the state table must name every `SubmissionState` and move every non-final one on; every `AuditAction` must have a row of the walk or name the Seam 1 test whose source asserts it (`SIGN_IN_FAILED`, `STEP_UP_FAILED`, `STEP_UP_FULL_SIGN_IN`, `FILE_REPLACED`, `PROPOSAL_WITHDRAWN`, `SMO_UNREACHABLE`, `SMO_UNREACHABLE_CLEARED`, `EXEMPTION_LAPSED`), and must have its label on the audit log screen (`frontend/src/api/audit.ts`); every endpoint Spring MVC maps for the application's controllers (every verb and path, class prefixes included) must have exactly one matrix row. `CompletenessSchemaTest` reads the CHECKs from PostgreSQL: `audit_entry_detail_check` must accept exactly the `DetailKey`s, and `va_submission_state_check` and `notification_state_check` exactly the `SubmissionState`s. The audit log is read in every suite through one harness reader, `AuditEntries`.

5. **What the check found, by the ticket that left it.**
   - The state table, the audit entries and the permission matrix: nothing the spec asks for in Release 1 was missing or wrong in what tickets 02 to 49 built.
   - The audit log screen's action filter (ticket 12's viewer, extended by ticket 48) named the actions up to ticket 28 only; the actions of tickets 30 (return, pass, returns read), 34 (endorse), 39 (SMO Unreachable), 40 and 42 (Exemptions), 41 (Cycle open and close, Not Endorsed), 43 (withdrawal), 44 (exports), 47 (role suspension, restore and review) and 49 (disposal) showed as raw codes and could not be chosen. They now have labels, and the guard in item 4 keeps it so.
   - The session store (tickets 02 and 09, seen in tickets 13 and 46): item 7.

6. **Reading the audit log is itself audited** (Q11, decided by the user on 2026-10-25, option (b); IM8 lm-4). Every answered `GET /api/audit-entries`, a VA POC's or a Cyber Manager's, writes one `AUDIT_LOG_VIEWED` entry in the read's transaction (`AuditLog.read`): by the reader in the roles they hold, with every filter asked for as detail (`filter_actor`, `filter_action`, `filter_system`, `filter_submission`, `filter_from`, `filter_to`) and the `page`; never anything an entry says, and no count. `AuditQuery` is both the filter and the entry, so the two cannot disagree. The entry is about no System or VA Submission: the filters are what the reader typed, not what the entry is about, and as the entry's ids they would put every read into the history it read (a Cyber Manager's own history included) and record unchecked ids as subjects (decided 2026-10-26, kept as built: the filter ids stay detail, not subject, docs/agents/reviewer-decisions.md). A refused read is the `REQUEST_REFUSED` of every refusal, and a `submission=` outside a Cyber Manager's scope stays an unaudited 404 (ADR 0014). The audit module names the reader through a new port, `AuditCaller`, which `access`'s `AuditActor` implements, so `audit` still depends on no module that writes to it (ADR 0038, amended). Migration V41 rebuilds `audit_entry_detail_check` from the union of every key before it (the last rebuild was V36) and the seven new ones. The rejected alternative, keeping reads unaudited as list reads are (reviewer decisions, 2026-10-06), leaves no record of who looked at the trail. Because every page read is a new newest entry, the Seam 1 reader (`AuditEntries.all`) keeps each entry once and leaves the reads out unless asked for them, so counting the log never changes the count.

7. **A person's sessions are read back whole** (the 401 after a step-up). Spring Session JDBC's query for a person's sessions has no `ORDER BY`, and its reader builds one session from each run of adjacent rows. Once the store holds many rows of one person (a long-running store, or the Seam 1 suite after a few minutes), PostgreSQL joins by hash, a session's attribute rows come back apart, and the reader keeps whichever part came last. The one-session rule (ADR 0008) then misses the "ended" mark on an older session, so that session stays live; at the person's next step-up the rule sees two live sessions and ends both, the current one included, and the next request answers `401`. In production that is a VA POC signed out at a step-up, and an older session that a newer sign-in should have ended still working. The fix is `SessionStoreConfig.SESSIONS_OF_A_PERSON`, Spring Session JDBC 4.0.5's query ordered by session id, set through a `SessionRepositoryCustomizer` ordered last (Spring Boot's own customizer sets the table name, which resets every query). `OneSessionRuleTest` fills the store with 150 sign-ins of one person, analyses the tables as autovacuum would, and then signs in and steps up ten times: before the fix the first step-up ended its own session. The stopgap in the Seam 1 reset that deleted every signed-in session (ticket 13) is removed, so the full suite exercises the rule as production does; without the fix four step-up tests fail there every run, which is how `ExemptionDecisionTest` came to fail twice in ticket 46.

Not done here, on purpose:
- Release 2: the Auditor's grant, scope and reads, the Cyber Manager's detailed export, the auditor pack, mid-cycle moves and transfers.
- Ticket 51: the production adapters (`adapters.pending`: identity status, file store, mail relay, malware scanner).
- Other reads stay unaudited as decided: the Register list, inboxes, boards, the role-holder review, and the export and disposal lists (reviewer decisions, ADRs 0019, 0056); a `404` and a `429` are not refusals the log records (ADR 0014).

Consequences:
- The suites add about four minutes to the back-end gate; `OneSessionRuleTest` about 45 seconds.
- A ticket that adds an endpoint, an audited action (with its screen label), a detail key or a state adds its row to the matching suite, or the build fails and says which.
- `MailSeam1.returnedForRework` moved up to `ReturnSeam1`, which the new suites share.
- The suites move the clock forward and leave audit entries dated later than the default day, so the Register tests that took the newest entry of an action as their own now read the log only up to their own business date (`AuditEntries.untilToday`), as `AuditSeam1` already does.

Amends ADR 0008 (how the one-session rule reads the store) and ADR 0038 (log reads are audited; the `AuditCaller` port).
